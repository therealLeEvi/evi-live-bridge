    How EVI Score V2 works, and what it is measured to be worth
    

      Six components, rescaled to total 100: about 29 points current net opportunity after tax, 24 liquidity,
      18 your personal history, 12 holding-time fit, 12 market stability and price freshness, 6 market-quality / crowding proxy.
      When a prediction has been loaded, the horizon matching the flip mode adjusts the score by up to ±6.
      Your history is shrunk toward a neutral prior so one lucky flip does not dominate a repeated winner, and an item you have
      never traded scores half of its 18 rather than zero — an unfamiliar item is not punished for being unfamiliar.
      Your own feedback moves it too: Great +2, Slow −3, Bad −7. A trade expected to be extremely slow loses 10.
      A negative after-tax margin is excluded outright.
    

    

      What changed on 26 September 2026, and why. V1 had a seventh component worth 15 points for a large
      expected absolute profit. It was measured and removed. It rewarded size, which on any given day means a big
      position in something illiquid, and it double-counted the minimum-profit setting you already control — turning a filter
      you can see into a bias you could not. Measured over 60 days of archived prices against the same alternatives, removing
      it improved every figure that matters:
    

    

      With it (V1) the median pick made 4,991 gp, won 53% of the time, left 55% of trades unsold, had a worst trade of
      −493,749, and took 55% of all its profit from a single trade. Without it (V2, what you are using) the median
      pick makes 14,085 gp, wins 71%, leaves 31% unsold, has a worst trade of −72,486, and its best single
      trade is 9% of the total — the profit is spread across many items instead of resting on one. Over 24-hour holds it
      wins 80% with 3% concentration. At a large cash stack the worst trade fell from −4,111,256 to −72,486.
    

    

      Picking at random is not the alternative, whatever its win rate suggests. Random wins about four times in five —
      on a median trade of a few hundred gp. Give it a real cash stack and count only trades expecting 500k or more and it
      loses 18 to 22 million. A win rate is worthless without the size of the win beside it.
    

    

      The score is still only the scanner's opinion, not the decision. A pick sent to the bridge is checked again there
      against things this score knows nothing about: the buy-limit windows, the volume cap for your trade duration, whether
      buyers have actually been paying your sell price, the item's own fill history, crash alerts and the correlation check.
      Any of those can demote or drop whatever is ranked first here. And the measurement above is one 60-day window with a
      fill model generous to every strategy equally, so treat the score as a shortlist, not a verdict and read the
      item's own numbers before committing. Rerun it yourself: node tools/score-v1-check.mjs.

Bring your Exchange Logger history


If you have been running the Exchange Logger plugin, EVI can read its logs and start with the trades you have already made, instead of knowing nothing about you until your first EVI trade. The files are read from your own .runelite folder, on this computer, and are never written to or sent anywhere. EVI matches them with its own FIFO matcher and its own tax model, exactly as it does its own trades.






Nothing is read until you ask, and nothing is added until you have seen the preview.
